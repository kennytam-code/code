## What the model does

The factor estimator models common market/sector risk using named ETF directions followed by equity PCA on the residual common variation of the declared stock scope. Auto compare can select this estimator or constrained return tracking using training-only comparisons. Only the first usable broad-market proxy per market supplies a named factor; its backups remain eligible hedge instruments without consuming extra factor slots ahead of the sector. The target stock is excluded from peer PCA. Factor loadings use orthogonal least-squares projection; the covariance includes shrunk residual co-movements and each instrument's remaining variance, so minimizing shared exposure cannot treat hedge-stock risk as free. This is a statistical risk model, not an expected-alpha model. Unexplained returns also contain company risk, omitted factors and noise. Minimizing variance does not impose exact market/sector neutrality; inspect the remaining exposures.

The basket is the primary result. Current single-stock, single-ETF and local-market ETF hedges remain references. Joint search screens complementary pairs across eligible names, grows combinations and replaces members; PCA, correlation and forward-selection recipes also remain competing procedures. Small universes screen all subsets; final constrained fits are applied to a shortlist. Larger searches are bounded, and actual screen/fit counts are reported. It does not claim a global optimum. More searching can increase fitting bias, so the historical tests remain separate.

The selected procedure is refitted through the latest analysis date, using the **CV-selected lookback for longer runs** or the full selected window for short holdout/in-sample runs. `weights`, `returns`, `current_fit` and headline in-sample R² describe that current fit. Earlier frozen validation fits and weights are retained separately in `validation_weights`, `validation_fit_returns` and `validation_hedge_alternatives`. Unseen scores test those historical procedures; they do not validate today's precise refitted weights.

The factor objective minimizes modelled spread variance, subject to gross/per-leg caps. This can choose a lower tracking R² than ordinary least squares. The report shows actual raw in-sample R², fitted volatility reduction, factor exposures before/after hedging, weight sensitivity and a separate fitted tracking-basket comparison. Same-name OLS bounds are detailed diagnostics, not the headline score or independently tested alternatives. Adjusted R² remains available as a nominal complexity heuristic; it is not an unbiased statistic after model selection or regularization.

The result diagnostics separate the same-name fit gap into exposure-cap effects (uncapped OLS minus capped OLS) and objective/regularization effects (capped OLS minus actual fitted R²), on identical dates. The capped reference permits zero legs, so it is a descriptive upper reference, not a selected basket or an unseen test. Short 1m runs also retain their descriptive candidate correlations.

Low fitted R² is still a limitation. Under the report's same-date fitted-intercept convention, fitted volatility reduction equals `1 - sqrt(1 - R²)`; these are not independent pieces of evidence. A low-tracking-R² hedge can reduce a particular common exposure when company-specific variation dominates, but fitted exposure reduction alone does not validate future effectiveness. Negative fitted R² means increased fitted spread variance under this convention and remains visible. Residual risk is not automatically alpha.

**Numerically negligible risk reductions do not earn a positive verdict.** Reductions below 0.05% (the one-decimal display boundary) are labelled no material validated reduction. This tolerance is not a cost hurdle or statistical significance test. Common-factor reduction is displayed alongside the fraction of target variance represented by those factors; near-100% factor reduction can still leave most stock risk.

**Negative scores remain visible.** Negative unseen tracking R² means a poor return-tracking test against its mean-return benchmark. It is not automatically equivalent to increased spread volatility, because tracking also penalizes mean-return errors. Variance/common-risk modes evaluate volatility evidence and report tracking separately; strict tracking mode retains a negative-R² rejection. No unseen observations means an unvalidated research candidate and empty `trade_weights`. `trade_eligible` is a legacy statistical-evidence flag, not order approval; execution checks always remain necessary.

Short windows usually provide descriptive fitted evidence; where enough data exist, a final segment is reserved before the current refit. Longer windows use chronological inner/outer validation when feasible. Missing target dates are diagnosed separately from constant returns. Data errors are not repaired by inventing returns, hiding losses, or forcing R² positive. Alternative baskets are distinct current-fitting-window research fits, not independent unseen winners.

## Controls and output

The form defaults to Auto compare, joint basket search, 2× maximum gross hedge exposure and 1× per instrument. The API preserves optional caps, so set them explicitly when scripting. A required range is inclusive; equal bounds request an exact count. All current and alternative baskets respect required/excluded names, scope and exposure caps. Every counted basket leg must have absolute notional of at least **1 per 100 units of target**. Dust recipes are rejected, not padded by forcing extra risk. This is a disclosed materiality convention, not statistical significance. Requested meaningful legs must be feasible; the model cannot fabricate a basket to meet an impossible request.

```python
result = find_best_hedge(

    sample_window='2m', candidate_scope='related', same_exchange_only=True,
    basket_size_range=(3, 5), alternative_baskets=5,

```

Charts lead the institutional report, using a white background, red accents, charcoal tables and Arial typography. It includes the current basket, raw fitted R² for every window, exposure reduction, multiple alternative recipes, single references, historical evidence, sensitivity and coverage/execution checks. Key fit and historical-test measures include a brief interpretation. Alternative positions and metrics appear together for comparison. Run displays one styled report in a notebook, without a second duplicate transcript. Terminal runs print the same report. `result["report_text"]` and exports retain the complete printable text. Position recipes use currency-neutral amounts per 100 target and explicit line breaks to avoid notebook math rendering swallowing dollar-delimited text. Compact and full reports use the same current weights; full adds configuration detail. Word and notebook reports share the same structured content. `diagnostic_log`/`full_report` preserve the legacy calculation log with its earlier-fit context. The design is inspired by institutional research reports; it does not represent an official Nomura document.

Optional window comparisons are overlapping historical fits, not independent repeated tests. Borrow availability is unknown unless supplied explicitly in `data['borrow_available']`; a confirmed unavailable short leg prevents eligibility. Bid/ask and average volume are snapshot diagnostics. Costs, financing, taxes, slippage, borrow fees and FX conversion are not modelled. Known mixed quote currencies, or a cross-market basket with unverified currencies, produce a prominent FX-normalization warning and empty `trade_weights`. Displayed research coefficients are not FX-adjusted order sizes; unknown currency is never assumed equal. Those local-currency fits cannot establish common-currency portfolio risk. Paths use a log-return spread approximation, not an exact self-financing portfolio backtest. Cross-time-zone two-day returns reduce timing mismatch but do not remove it.

`quick_rehedge` pins the selected recipe rule and basket size, including joint-search ranks in longer validation windows. Reapplying that rule can select different names on new data; it does not hold the previous constituents fixed or transfer the old validation to today's weights.
