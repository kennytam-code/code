MARKET-MOVING STOCKS: US AND ASIA WATCHLIST

Reviewed 11 October 2026, Hong Kong time.

Purpose: a concise recurring watchlist for a senior's market briefing, covering the US, Taiwan,
Japan, Hong Kong, mainland China, Australia and South Korea. The priorities below are editorial
judgment informed by the linked index, holdings and company sources. This is a monitoring
universe; it does not identify which stocks are moving the market on any particular day.

SELECTION LOGIC

Each company earns its place through one or more of these channels:

1. Index influence: a substantial benchmark weight means its share-price movement can
   materially affect the index. For illustration, a stock with a 10% index weight rising 5%
   contributes approximately 0.5 percentage points to the index, holding other constituents
   unchanged.
2. Sector spillover: earnings, guidance or investment decisions can change expectations for
   suppliers, competitors or stocks overseas.
3. Economic signal: results provide useful information about consumption, credit, interest
   rates, exports or commodity demand.
4. Distinct information: avoid giving several companies separate briefing slots when they are
   delivering essentially the same sector signal.

Index importance and economic usefulness are different reasons for inclusion. A company
selected as a credit or consumption indicator should not automatically receive the same
priority as an index heavyweight. ETF holdings are supporting evidence of market prominence;
their weights are not the weights of the local benchmark.

REVIEWED SHORTLIST

Tickers identify the local share listings. Hong Kong means HK-listed shares, including mainland
businesses; the mainland China row uses A-shares. Alphabet's two US share classes count as one
company in this watchlist. There are 40 companies across the seven markets.

US
Highest briefing priority: Nvidia (NVDA), Microsoft (MSFT), Apple (AAPL), Amazon (AMZN),
Alphabet (GOOGL / GOOG), Meta (META), Tesla (TSLA), Broadcom (AVGO), Micron (MU)
Other important names: JPMorgan Chase (JPM), as a banking and credit indicator
Logic: The Mag 7 combine index influence with sector leadership. Broadcom and Micron add
distinct semiconductor signals. JPMorgan broadens the information set beyond technology.

TAIWAN
Highest briefing priority: TSMC (2330)
Other important names: Hon Hai / Foxconn (2317), MediaTek (2454), Delta Electronics (2308)
Logic: TSMC combines market size with global chip-supply-chain influence. The other three cover
electronics/server manufacturing, chip design and power infrastructure.

Other important names: Xiaomi (1810), Meituan (3690), especially for sector news
Logic: Combine major index constituents with China internet/AI sentiment and financial-sector

AUSTRALIA

considered together when the news is sector-wide
Logic: CBA covers domestic banking, housing and rates; BHP covers commodities and China demand.

Highest briefing priority: Samsung Electronics (005930), SK Hynix (000660)
Other important names: Hyundai Motor (005380)
Logic: Samsung and SK Hynix combine market concentration with global memory/AI influence.
Hyundai adds a different signal through autos and exports.


WHY THESE CHOICES

US

- Nvidia: AI computing demand and the spending outlook for the wider AI supply chain.
- Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet: cloud demand, AI investment and monetisation. Alphabet also
  provides advertising information.
- Meta: digital advertising and AI infrastructure spending.
- Apple: consumer devices and the electronics supply chain.
- Tesla: EV competition and investor sentiment around autonomy and growth expectations; its
  signal differs from the cloud and semiconductor companies.
- Broadcom: networking and custom-chip exposure, which complements Nvidia's computing signal.
- Micron: memory demand and pricing, including high-bandwidth memory used in AI infrastructure.
- JPMorgan: banking, credit and consumer/business conditions. Its role here is a financial-
  sector indicator, not an assertion that its index impact equals Nvidia's.

The S&P 500 page's 31 August 2026 constituent snapshot lists the Mag 7, Broadcom and Micron in
its ten largest constituent positions, counting Alphabet's two share classes separately. The
choice is therefore supported by index prominence as well as thematic relevance. S&P 500
constituents [1], Micron AI memory and storage [2], JPMorgan business profile [3].

TAIWAN

TSMC comes first. Hon Hai adds manufacturing/server exposure; MediaTek adds chip design and
handset demand; Delta adds power infrastructure. These four were Taiwan's largest listed
companies by market capitalisation at end-2025. That historical size ranking supports their
inclusion but is not a current TAIEX weight calculation. Taiwan Stock Exchange 2026 fact book
[4].

JAPAN

Advantest, Tokyo Electron, Fast Retailing and SoftBank Group were the Nikkei's four largest
constituents by weight in the 8 October 2026 daily summary. Fast Retailing earns its place
through index influence even though its business has less semiconductor spillover. Toyota and
MUFG provide broader economic information and appear among the prominent holdings in the Japan
ETF factsheet reviewed. SoftBank Group means 9984, distinct from the listed telecom company
SoftBank Corp. Nikkei daily summary [5], iShares Japan factsheet [6].

HONG KONG

Tencent and Alibaba combine index influence with internet/AI sentiment; HSBC and AIA add
financial-sector coverage. CCB is an addition after review: omitting a major constituent
weakened the earlier shortlist's ability to explain the wider market. Xiaomi and Meituan remain
useful sector leaders but should receive more attention when their particular sectors have
consequential news. Hang Seng constituent information [7], Hang Seng November 2025 constituent
review [8].

MAINLAND CHINA

CATL and BYD cover different parts of the EV ecosystem: batteries and vehicle competition.
Moutai is a premium-consumption indicator, while China Merchants Bank adds credit and banking
information. Zhongji Innolight adds AI optical networking. Its inclusion is supported by its
prominent position in the A-share ETF factsheet reviewed, although that does not establish its
precise CSI 300 weight or prove that a daily move causes the whole market to move. iShares
China A factsheet [9], BYD company profile [10].

AUSTRALIA

The shortlist reflects the importance of banks and resources in the Australian market. CBA and
BHP receive first attention. NAB, Westpac and ANZ collectively matter, but requiring four
separate bank updates every day would duplicate information when the driver is a shared rate,
housing or credit development. Separate them when earnings or company-specific news creates a
meaningful difference. This compact selection deliberately favours the dominant bank/resource
exposures; it is not comprehensive sector coverage. S&P research on Australian index
concentration [11], ASX-hosted 2026 market overview [12].

SOUTH KOREA

Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix deserve much more attention than the other Korean names
because of their market prominence and shared global memory/AI relevance. Hyundai is included
to add an auto/export signal rather than another semiconductor exposure. The Korea ETF
factsheet supports the large difference in prominence between the semiconductor pair and
Hyundai; its fund weights should not be presented as KOSPI weights. iShares Korea factsheet
[13].

DAILY BRIEFING RULE

Elevate a company when its news changes expectations for the wider index or sector. Prioritise
earnings/guidance surprises, investment plans, demand or pricing changes, and major regulatory
or policy developments. A TSMC outlook change or a memory-pricing signal may matter more than a
list of routine daily share-price moves.

Use this note as the recurring monitoring universe. For each daily briefing, confirm the actual
news, price movement and benchmark contribution before attributing that day's market direction
to a company. The linked data snapshots have different observation dates; the review date is
not a claim that all source figures are current to 11 October 2026.
